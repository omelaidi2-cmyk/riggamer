What to Do in Your First 3 Hours (Exact Order)

The tutorial runs approximately 90 minutes and covers combat, base setup, and your first operator pulls. After it ends, execute this sequence before anything else:

  1. Open mailbox immediately. Claim Ardelia, all bonus Orundum packages, and launch window gifts. Some expire after 7 days from account creation. First action, every time, no exceptions.
  2. Activate all base production facilities. The tutorial unlocks them but does not turn them on automatically. Go to your base, activate the Originium Lab, Trading Post, and Power Station. These generate passive Orundum while you are offline. Every hour they sit idle is Orundum lost permanently.
  3. Complete daily missions before logging off. They reset at midnight UTC. Week-one dailies offer first-completion bonuses that standard dailies do not. Run them before you do anything else in the main game.
  4. Do not pull yet. You have not seen the full banner rotation. Your week-one income plus achievement completions will give you 38–66 pulls. Spend them on the right banner, not the first one you see. Read the tier list first.

Choosing Your Starter — Which Pick Sets You Up Best

Shortly into the tutorial, the game gives you a permanent choice of starter operator. This decision matters more than most guides admit. Here is the honest breakdown:

AoE Caster — best pick for most new players. Covers grouped enemies without precise positioning, pairs directly with Ardelia's support kit, and clears early content without requiring a healer babysit. If you are not sure what you want, pick the Caster and do not overthink it.

Damage Dealer — best pick if you plan to pull a support operator early. Highest single-target output, but needs sustain. Only take this if your first pull target from the tier list is a healer or support operator.

Support — best pick if you have read the tier list and have a specific DPS pull target. Amplifies your team significantly but feels underwhelming solo. Do not pick this as your first decision before you know what operators you are pulling toward.

Defender — avoid for most new players. High survivability but low damage creates slow clears. The game rewards aggressive play. A Defender starter extends your time-to-progress unnecessarily.

The Base System — Why It Makes Endfield Different

No other major gacha on PC right now has a passive resource generation system tied to real-time production facilities. This is not a minor feature — it is the structural reason Endfield's F2P income beats Genshin's by 40–50% per week.

Your base on Talos-II runs three core production chains: Originium processing, material refinement, and Orundum generation through the Trading Post. Set up correctly during the tutorial, your base generates roughly 400 Orundum per week without you doing anything beyond logging in to collect.

The trap new players fall into: the tutorial builds the base for you, they treat it as a cutscene, and never activate production. Those players lose 400 Orundum per week — roughly 2 extra pulls every week — for as long as they play. Over three months that is approximately 5,200 Orundum or 29 pulls simply abandoned.

Activate production on day one. Collect when you log in. That is the entire maintenance cost of the system that separates Endfield from every other gacha in passive F2P income.
